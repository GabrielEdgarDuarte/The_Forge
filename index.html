The hammer
that never tires.

For thousands of years, every blow came from a human arm. Then smiths learned to let water, steam and electricity swing the hammer for them.

  1. Antiquity

    Roman water wheels

    Roman engineers used water wheels to drive mills, and some of them may already have powered simple hammers.

  2. Middle Ages

    Trip hammers

    Water-powered trip hammers spread through Europe’s forges, beating raw iron into bars far faster than any arm.

  3. Industrial age

    The steam hammer

    James Nasmyth’s steam hammer forged huge parts for ships and locomotives with precise, controlled blows.

  4. Today

    Air and electricity

    Compact electric and air-powered hammers sit in small workshops, where smiths still use them every day.
